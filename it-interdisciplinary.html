Segment Overview

The Information Technology Segment invites students of Classes 11 and 12 to submit and present original research papers addressing a real-world problem within the domain of Cybersecurity and Digital Infrastructure. The segment comprises two tracks – Technical and Interdisciplinary – each inviting a different mode of inquiry into the same broad theme of Technology and Society.

  • Each participating school may nominate one team comprising a maximum of 2 students for the Technical and/or the Interdisciplinary tracks.
  • Participants of one track cannot be repeated in the other.
  • This segment will be non-competitive.
  • The format of submission will be the IEEE format, as provided by the Host School. Download the template →
  • The word limit for all research papers will be 3000–3500 words, excluding the abstract. The abstract should be within 500 words.
  • It should include problem identification → research → solution → evaluation.
  • Please note that once the Expression of Interest Form is filled up, a separate Registration Form will be sent out to the schools. The details of the participants must be filled in the Registration Form. No changes will be accepted after the form has been submitted.

Themes & Scope

Each team must develop their paper around one of the following themes.

Comparative Case Analysis

Examine two contrasting cases to draw out a broader argument about the chosen theme. Use the comparison to test or refine a claim.

Theoretical Application

Apply an established theory or framework from the chosen discipline (a philosophical account of agency, a theory of state power, a sociological theory of trust, etc.) to a contemporary AI or cybersecurity issue. Assess how well it holds up.

Policy or Ethical Critique

Critically evaluate an existing law, policy or institutional stance related to AI, data privacy or cybersecurity. Identify its underlying assumptions and propose a reasoned alternative grounded in the discipline’s theory.

Suggested Questions

  • With artificial intelligence increasingly taking over decisions in surveillance and digital infrastructure, does anything remain uniquely human about human thought, agency or consciousness?
  • How is public trust in institutions (media, government, banking, etc.) changing as surveillance, data collection and algorithmic decision-making expand into everyday life?
  • What can earlier periods of rapid technological disruption (the Industrial Revolution, the early Internet, etc.) teach us about how digital infrastructure and governance adapt, or fail to adapt, to the AI era?
  • ‘Digital Divide 2.0’ – does the rapid development of digital infrastructure and AI lessen social inequality, or does it lead to new kinds of technical exclusion?
  • What obligations should technology platforms have, and who should enforce them, to prevent deepfakes, misinformation and AI-generated content from undermining democratic decision-making?

Research Paper Requirements

Each paper must include the following elements:

  • Problem Statement – A clear explanation of the problem being addressed, supported by evidence.
  • Primary Research – The paper should be argument and evidence driven rather than data/methodology driven. Primary data is optional.
  • Secondary Research – Information collected from credible sources such as industry reports, government publications, academic studies and reliable news sources.
  • The technology dimension should be central to the paper.
  • It should build a clear, defensible thesis and engage substantively with existing scholarship in the chosen discipline, not merely offer opinion.
  • May use primary evidence where it strengthens the argument (interviews, case studies of specific policies or events, or small opinion surveys) as support, rather than as the core method.

Structural Template

  1. Abstract
  2. Introduction & Thesis Statement
  3. Disciplinary Framework / Theoretical Grounding
  4. Argument & Analysis (with supporting evidence or case studies)
  5. Counterarguments & Their Rebuttal
  6. Broader Implications
  7. Conclusion
  8. References & Appendices (if applicable)
