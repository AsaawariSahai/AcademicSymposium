Segment Overview

The Information Technology Segment invites students of Classes 11 and 12 to submit and present original research papers addressing a real-world problem within the domain of Cybersecurity and Digital Infrastructure. The segment comprises two tracks – Technical and Interdisciplinary – each inviting a different mode of inquiry into the same broad theme of Technology and Society.

  • Each participating school may nominate one team comprising a maximum of 2 students for the Technical and/or the Interdisciplinary tracks.
  • Participants of one track cannot be repeated in the other.
  • This segment will be non-competitive.
  • The format of submission will be the IEEE format, as provided by the Host School. Download the template →
  • The word limit for all research papers will be 3000–3500 words, excluding the abstract. The abstract should be within 500 words.
  • It should include problem identification → research → solution → evaluation.
  • Please note that once the Expression of Interest Form is filled up, a separate Registration Form will be sent out to the schools. The details of the participants must be filled in the Registration Form. No changes will be accepted after the form has been submitted.

Themes & Scope

Each team must develop their paper around one of the following themes.

Threat & Risk Analysis

Examine a real-world cyberattack or security incident. Evaluate its causes, impact, and what it reveals about gaps in preparedness or response.

Case Study of Sectoral Impact

Analyse how a specific cyber threat or vulnerability class affects a particular sector or user group – schools, small businesses, healthcare, public services. Identify structural gaps in awareness, process or technical controls.

Solution-Oriented Research Paper

Identify a specific cybersecurity or data-privacy challenge and propose a credible, evidence-backed, feasible technical or procedural solution, grounded in recognised frameworks or standards (e.g., NIST, ISO 27001) where relevant.

Suggested Questions

  • How well do different types of multi-factor authentication (push notifications, biometrics, etc.) resist common attacks like SIM-swapping or session hijacking?
  • How well do current phishing-detection tools (email filters, URL checks, ML-based classifiers) catch attacks designed to fool humans, not just machines?
  • Focusing on any one specific type of IoT device (e.g., smart home cameras), how do weak encryption or insecure APIs leave it open to attack?
  • Choose a specific popular app or platform – do their data-collection practices actually match their stated privacy policy?
  • How well do Indian institutions’ technical security measures (network segmentation, access controls, encryption) meet recognised cybersecurity standards?

Research & Submission Requirements

The paper must include the following elements:

  • Problem Statement – A clear explanation of the problem being addressed, supported by evidence.
  • Primary Research – Clear primary data to defend the argument.
  • Secondary Research – Information collected from credible sources such as industry reports, government publications, academic studies and reliable news sources.

Structural Template

  1. Executive Summary
  2. Introduction & Issue Framing
  3. Literature Review
  4. Methodology
  5. Findings & Analysis (Data-Driven)
  6. Current Practice or Policy Response (only if relevant)
  7. Proposed Solutions with Feasibility Assessment
  8. Limitations & Counterarguments
  9. Conclusion with Predicted Outcomes of the Research
  10. References & Appendices
