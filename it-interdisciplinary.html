IT Research Paper · Segment 1 · Track 2

Interdisciplinary Paper

An argument-driven thesis paper on Cybersecurity & Digital Infrastructure, in conversation with another discipline, under the overarching theme of Technology and Society.

Grades 11 & 12 Preferred format: IEEE Certifying partner: CSIR – IICB 6 Finalists

Event Overview

  • The symposium invites senior school students (Grades 11 and 12) to submit and present an interdisciplinary, research-based thesis paper.
  • It addresses a real-world problem within Cybersecurity and Digital Infrastructure while including an element from another domain of the student’s choice, under the overarching theme of Technology and Society.
  • Preferred format — IEEE format.
  • CSIR – Indian Institute of Chemical Biology is the named academic partner and certifying authority for this track, lending subject-matter credibility and a formal evaluation panel to the students’ work.

About the Papers

  • Should build a clear, defensible thesis and engage substantively with existing scholarship in the chosen discipline — not merely offer opinion.
  • Should be argument and evidence-driven rather than data/methodology-driven: engage seriously with disciplinary theory and existing scholarship to build a reasoned position, rather than run an experiment or survey.
  • Should keep the technology dimension central to the question, distinguishing the track from a general essay competition.
  • May use primary evidence — interviews, case studies of specific policies or events, or small opinion surveys — as support, rather than as the core method.

Suggested Approaches

Comparative Case Analysis

Examine two contrasting cases to draw out a broader argument about the chosen theme, using the comparison to test or refine a claim.

Theoretical Application

Apply an established theory or framework from the chosen discipline — a philosophical account of agency, a theory of state power, a sociological theory of trust — to a contemporary AI or cybersecurity issue, and assess how well it holds up.

Policy or Ethical Critique

Critically evaluate an existing law, policy or institutional stance related to AI, data privacy or cybersecurity. Identify its underlying assumptions and propose a reasoned alternative grounded in the discipline’s theory.

Structural Template

  1. Abstract
  2. Introduction & Thesis Statement
  3. Disciplinary Framework / Theoretical Grounding
  4. Argument & Analysis (with supporting evidence or case studies)
  5. Counterarguments & Their Rebuttal
  6. Broader Implications
  7. Conclusion
  8. References & Appendices (if applicable)

Suggested Questions

  • With artificial intelligence increasingly taking over decisions in surveillance and digital infrastructure, does anything remain uniquely human about human thought, agency or consciousness?
  • How is public trust in institutions (media, government, banking, etc.) changing as surveillance, data collection and algorithmic decision-making expand into everyday life?
  • What can earlier periods of rapid technological disruption (the Industrial Revolution, the early Internet, etc.) teach us about how digital infrastructure and governance adapt, or fail to adapt, to the AI era?
  • ‘Digital Divide 2.0’ — does the rapid development of digital infrastructure and AI lessen social inequality, or does it lead to new kinds of technical exclusion?
  • What obligations should technology platforms have, and who should enforce them, to prevent deepfakes, misinformation and AI-generated content from undermining democratic decision-making?
